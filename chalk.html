Climbing chalk

Chalk keeps your hands dry and your grip consistent. The right kind depends on how and where you climb.

What to look for

Type

Loose, chunky, ball or liquid. Each has a different mix of coverage, mess and convenience.

Dust

Some gyms limit loose chalk to keep the air clean, so check your gym’s rules first.

Carrying

A chalk bag on your waist, a bucket for bouldering, or a refillable chalk ball.

Skin

Chalk dries your hands. Look after your skin, and wash and moisturise after sessions.

Types of chalk

Loose chalk

Fine powder with the most coverage. It is also the messiest.

Chunky chalk

Crushed pieces that make less dust and can be broken down as you need.

Chalk ball

A refillable sock that releases chalk as you squeeze, with less mess.

Liquid chalk

A base layer that lasts longer and makes less airborne dust. Let it dry before climbing.

Which one for your level

Beginner

A chalk ball or liquid chalk, which are often gym-friendly. You may not need much at all.

Intermediate

Try liquid chalk as a base and loose chalk for hard attempts, if your gym allows it.

Advanced

Build a routine with the type you trust, and brush holds clean as you climb.

Tips

  • Check what your gym allows before you buy.
  • Keep your chalk in a bag you can close, to avoid spills.
  • Pair chalk with a soft brush to keep holds clean.
  • Moisturise your hands after sessions.

Common mistakes

  • Using too much, which can fill the skin’s texture and make holds slippery.
  • Chalking up out of habit instead of when your hands are damp.
  • Ignoring your gym’s chalk rules.
  • Leaving chalk on holds. Brush them clean for the next climber.
