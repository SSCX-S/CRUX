Intermediate guide

Break through the plateau.

You’re past the basics. At V3 to V5, progress comes less from effort and more from precision, power and smarter practice.

V0V1V2V3V4V5V6V7V8V9

This guide is for climbers around V3 to V5. Grades vary between gyms, so go by how the climbing feels.

What changes at this level

  • Moves get more complex: hooks, compression and dynamic movement replace simple pulling.
  • Holds are smaller and rests are rarer, so body position matters more.
  • When you fall, it’s usually technique or positioning, not strength alone.
  • Recovery and session structure start to decide how fast you improve.

Skills to build

Heel and toe hooks

Use your feet to pull in and take weight off your arms, especially on steeper walls.

Drop knees and flagging

Turn your hips side-on to reach further, and use a free leg for balance.

Controlled dynamic moves

Use your legs and a precise burst of momentum to reach, instead of pulling slowly on straight arms.

Compression

Squeeze between holds with your hands and feet to stay on the wall, especially on slopers and volumes.

Core tension

Keep your feet on the wall as you move between holds by engaging your core.

Open-hand grip

Use your whole hand on slopers. Crimping every hold puts more strain on your fingers.

Why plateaus happen

  • 01
    You climb the same styleSeek out the angle or hold type you avoid, such as steep, slab, slopers or crimps.
  • 02
    Every session is at your limitMix easier volume with a few focused attempts at your limit.
  • 03
    You skip restTake rest days, and rest properly between hard attempts.
  • 04
    You repeat mistakes unseenFilm some attempts and watch your feet, hips and the point where you stall.

A simple weekly structure

A template for three sessions a week. Adjust it to how your body feels.

  • Session A
    Technique and volumeWarm up, then climb plenty of problems a grade or two below your limit, with one skill in mind.
  • Session B
    ProjectingPick one or two problems at your limit. Work the hard sections and rest a few minutes between attempts.
  • Session C
    Your weaknessChoose the style you avoid and repeat easier problems in it until it feels natural.
  • Rest
    RecoveryTake at least one or two full rest days each week. Breakthroughs often come after rest.

Gear and training

  • Shoes: a moderately downturned pair can help on steeper climbing. Keep a flatter, comfortable pair for long volume sessions.
  • Finger training: many coaches suggest climbing consistently for a year or more first. If you start, get guidance, build up gradually and keep rest days.
  • Skin and recovery: look after your skin, sleep well and treat rest as part of training.

Sharp or localised finger pain is a reason to stop and rest. If it doesn’t settle, see a physio or doctor.

Signs you’re ready for Advanced

  • You can read and sequence most problems in your grade before touching the wall.
  • You climb different styles with confidence, not just your favourite.
  • You know your weaknesses and train them on purpose.
  • You recover well and your sessions follow a plan.
