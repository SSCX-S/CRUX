This guide is for climbers at V6 and up. Grades vary between gyms, so go by how the climbing feels.

What changes at this level

  • Margins are small, so details in your feet, hips and timing decide attempts.
  • Progress is slower and more specific, so random sessions stop working.
  • Your tendons take longer to adapt than your muscles, and finger injuries become more common as loads rise.
  • Recovery, consistency and mindset matter as much as raw strength.

Skills to refine

Precise body tension

Keep your whole body engaged so your feet stay on and your hips stay close, even on steep or small holds.

Coordination and dynamic moves

Practise dynos, catches and coordination moves with control, so you can commit and stick the landing.

Strong pulling and lock-offs

Build the pulling strength to hold positions and move from them without wasting energy.

Efficient beta

Look for the sequence that costs the least. A smaller, smarter move often beats a bigger, stronger one.

Matching grip to hold

Use the grip that suits each hold: open-hand, half crimp or pinch, and avoid over-crimping when you don’t need to.

Mental commitment

Decide, breathe and go. Visualise the moves, manage fear and treat every fall as information.

Training with structure

A template for three or four sessions a week. Adjust it to your body and your goals.

  • Session A
    Limit boulderingShort, focused attempts at your hardest problems, with full rests between goes.
  • Session B
    Strength and powerTargeted pulling, power and finger work, ideally from a structured programme or coach.
  • Session C
    Technique and weaknessesWork the styles and moves that hold you back, at a lower intensity.
  • Easy week
    RecoveryEvery few weeks, drop the volume and intensity so your body can catch up.

For finger and strength work, a coach or a proven programme is worth it. Build load gradually and keep rest days.

How to work a project

  • Pick a project that suits your strengths, and one that challenges a weakness.
  • Break it into sections, then link them together.
  • Rest several minutes between hard attempts. Quality matters more than attempt count.
  • Keep a log or film your attempts so you can see what is improving.
  • Know when to walk away and come back fresh.

Recovery and injury prevention

  • Sleep well and treat rest days as part of your training.
  • Balance all that pulling with pushing exercises and shoulder stability work.
  • Warm up thoroughly before hard attempts, and stop when your fingers feel sharp or sore.

Sharp or localised finger pain is a reason to stop and rest. If it doesn’t settle, see a physio or doctor.

Gear

  • Shoes: consider a specialised pair, such as a more aggressive shoe for steep climbs and a softer one for smearing, and get worn soles resoled.
  • Chalk, brush and tape: keep your skin in good condition and your holds clean.
  • Training log: a simple record of sessions helps you see what works.

Where to go next

  • Consider a coach for a plan and fresh eyes on your technique.
  • If you move outdoors, go with experienced climbers and bring crash pads and spotters.
  • Set goals beyond a grade, such as a style, a project or a competition.
